# Digital Recommendation Letter (Sifaris): Feasibility Study

*A self-initiated case study by Basant Tamang.*

Ward offices in Nepal issue recommendation letters (sifaris) on paper. Each needs the ward chairperson's signature, and interviews found that citizens often return because "the boss is not there to sign". The offices that receive these letters, such as banks, can't easily check they are genuine.

This study asks whether letters should go digital, compares four options with a weighted scoring model, tests how sensitive the result is, and recommends a phased approach.

> **In simple words:** before anyone spends money building something, a feasibility study asks "is it worth it, and which way is best?" I compared four ways to fix the problem, scored them, checked how sure the result is, and recommended a cheap fix now plus a small trial of the digital option.

## Recommendation

1. **Now:** numbered paper letters with a register in every ward (cheap, quick)
2. **Pilot:** QR-verifiable digital letters, signed remotely by the chairperson, in one ward for 3 months
3. **Then decide:** roll out to all 32 wards only if the pilot meets its targets

The digital option scored highest (3.75 out of 5), but only just ahead of the paper option (3.60), and its advantage depends on problems not yet confirmed. So a pilot comes before any full rollout.
